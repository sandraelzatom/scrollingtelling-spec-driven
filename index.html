1950s – 1960s: The Genesis

The First Wave

1950: Alan Turing publishes "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," proposing the Turing Test.

1956: The term "Artificial Intelligence" is coined at Dartmouth. Optimism is high that human-like intelligence is imminent.

1966: MIT’s Joseph Weizenbaum creates ELIZA, the first chatterbot.

1970s: The First AI Winter

The Great Stagnation

Early programs struggle with "common sense." Public interest collapses as computers prove too slow and data too scarce.

Government funding is slashed; AI research enters a period of hibernation.

1980s: Resurgence

Expert Systems

AI simulates human experts in medical and chemical domains. Backpropagation is developed, allowing multi-layer neural networks to learn.

1990s – 2000s: Symbolic Triumph

Deep Blue to Big Data

1997: IBM’s Deep Blue defeats world chess champion Garry Kasparov.

2000s: The Internet creates massive datasets (Big Data), fueling modern machine learning algorithms.

2010s: The Turning Point

Deep Learning Boom

2012: AlexNet revolutionizes image classification at the ImageNet competition.

2016: DeepMind’s AlphaGo defeats world champion Lee Sedol at the game of Go.

2020s – Present

Generative & Agentic AI

The rise of Transformer architectures and Large Language Models (LLMs).

Shift from data analysis to Creation: text, images, code, and Agentic systems capable of reasoning.
