1950s – 1960s: The First Wave

The Genesis

1950: Alan Turing publishes "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," proposing the Turing Test.

1956: The term "Artificial Intelligence" is coined at Dartmouth by John McCarthy. Pioneers like Minsky and Newell predict human-like intelligence is imminent.

1966: MIT’s Joseph Weizenbaum creates ELIZA, the world's first chatterbot.

1970s: The First AI Winter

The Great Stagnation

Early programs struggle with "common sense" and real-world complexity.

Funding droughts begin as computers are too slow and data is too scarce to support the grand promises of early researchers.

1980s: Expert Systems

Resurgence

AI finds new life simulating human experts in specific domains like medical diagnoses and chemical analysis.

Early neural networks and the "backpropagation" algorithm are developed, allowing multi-layer networks to learn.

1990s – 2000s: The Pivot

Symbolic Triumph

1997: IBM’s Deep Blue defeats world chess champion Garry Kasparov.

2000s: The widespread adoption of the internet creates "Big Data," providing the necessary fuel for machine learning to adapt and train.

2010s: The Turning Point

Deep Learning Boom

2012: AlexNet revolutionizes image classification, sparking the "Deep Learning" revolution.

2016: Google DeepMind’s AlphaGo defeats world champion Lee Sedol at the game of Go.

2020s – Present

Generative Explosion

The rise of Transformer architectures and Large Language Models (LLMs).

AI transitions from analyzing data to Creating original text, images, and code.

Today's focus: Agentic AI and multimodal systems that interpret text, audio, and visual info seamlessly.
