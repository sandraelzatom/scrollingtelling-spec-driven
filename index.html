1950s – 1960s: The Genesis

The First Wave

1950: Alan Turing publishes "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," proposing the Turing Test.

1956: The term "Artificial Intelligence" is coined at Dartmouth by John McCarthy, Minsky, and Newell.

1966: MIT’s Joseph Weizenbaum creates ELIZA, the first rule-based chatterbot.

1970s: The First AI Winter

The Great Stagnation

Early programs struggle with "common sense" and real-world complexity.

Funding droughts begin as computers are too slow and data is too scarce to support grand promises.

1980s: Resurgence

Expert Systems

AI finds life simulating human experts in specific domains like medical diagnoses and chemical analysis.

Backpropagation is developed, allowing multi-layer neural networks to learn for the first time.

1990s – 2000s: Symbolic Triumph

The Big Data Pivot

1990s: Commercial expert systems become too expensive, leading to a brief Second Winter.

1997: IBM’s Deep Blue defeats Garry Kasparov, proving rule-based strategy mastery.

2000s: The Internet creates massive datasets ("Big Data"), fueling modern machine learning algorithms.

2010s: Deep Learning Boom

The Turning Point

2012: AlexNet achieves an unprecedented leap in image classification at the ImageNet competition.

2016: DeepMind’s AlphaGo defeats world champion Lee Sedol at the game of Go.

2020s – Present

The Generative Explosion

Rise of Transformer architectures and Large Language Models (LLMs).

Shift from data analysis to Creation: text, images, code, and video.

Focus moves to Agentic AI and multimodal systems interpreting text, audio, and visuals.
